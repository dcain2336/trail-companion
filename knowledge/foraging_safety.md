# Foraging Safety (reference — distilled from field guides)

## Golden rules
1. NEVER eat a wild plant or mushroom unless a local expert has verified it in person.
2. Learn the poisonous lookalikes FIRST, the edibles second.
3. When in doubt, throw it out. No meal is worth organ failure.
4. Avoid foraging near roadsides, industrial sites, or sprayed fields (chemical contamination).
5. Take only what you need; leave enough for wildlife and regrowth.

## Critical poisonous lookalikes (Eastern US)
- **Poison hemlock** looks like **wild carrot / Queen Anne's lace**. Hemlock has smooth, purple-blotched hollow stems and a mousy smell; wild carrot has hairy stems and one dark floret in the center. Hemlock kills in small doses.
- **Destroying angel / death cap (Amanita)** look like **edible Agaricus / puffballs**. Amanitas have white gills, a volva (cup at base), and a ring on the stem. Rule: never eat a white-gilled mushroom unless verified.
- **False morels** look like **true morels**. True morels are hollow inside cap-to-stem like a honeycomb; false morels are chambered or solid with a wrinkled, brain-like cap.
- **Wild iris / blue flag** roots look like **cattail shoots** in early spring. Iris causes severe GI distress.
- **Pokeweed shoots** resemble **asparagus** when young. Only very young shoots (<6 inches) prepared by boiling twice are used traditionally; mature plant is toxic.

## Beginner-safe species (still verify locally)
- Dandelion (leaves, flowers, roots) — unmistakable once flowering.
- Cattail (shoots, pollen, roots) — the "supermarket of the swamp."
- Blackberries / raspberries — aggregate berries on thorny canes; avoid white berries generally.
- Chicken of the woods (mushroom) — bright orange/yellow shelf fungus on wood; no deadly lookalike, but verify.

## Mushroom safety rules
1. Check spore print color.
2. Never rely on a single field mark.
3. Avoid all little brown mushrooms (LBMs) as a beginner.
4. Cook all wild mushrooms thoroughly.
5. Save an uncooked sample of anything you eat (for ER identification).
6. Eat only a small portion first; wait 24 hours.
7. Join a local mycological society before eating anything.
