# Camping Basics (reference)

## Campsite selection
- **Drainage:** never camp in depressions or dry creek beds (flash floods). Look for slightly elevated, flat ground.
- **Wind:** pitch the tent's narrow end into the prevailing wind; use natural windbreaks.
- **Water:** camp 200+ feet from lakes and streams (regulation + hygiene).
- **Hazards:** scan for widowmakers (dead branches overhead), ant mounds, game trails, flood lines on trees.
- **Bear country:** cook and store food 100+ yards downwind of the tent; use a bear canister or hang.

## Shelter
- Dome tent: standard 3-season; stake all guylines in wind.
- Tarp: lighter, needs practice; A-frame or lean-to pitch.
- Bivy: minimal; condensation management matters.
- No stakes? Use deadman anchors: bury sticks/rocks tied to guylines.

## Camp kitchen
- Water: 1 gallon per person per day minimum (drinking + cooking).
- Layout: kitchen triangle — stove, prep, wash — away from tent.
- Bear country: nothing scented in the tent, ever.

## Weather contingencies
- Rain: trench around tent (where permitted), keep gear off the ground, have dry clothes sealed.
- Cold: layer system (base/mid/shell), no cotton ("cotton kills"), vent the tent to fight condensation.
- Heat: shade, electrolytes, siesta during peak sun.
- Wind: low profile, extra guylines, park vehicles as windbreaks.
